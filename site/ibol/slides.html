THE TRANSACTION

Different legal objects emerge

01

Premium payment

A payment discharges the premium obligation.

02

Policy rights

The insurer’s promise creates contractual rights.

03

Security

The instrument identifies the lender’s rights and recourse.

04

Company credit

Drawdown creates a debt and a new account asset.

Economic continuity makes the history relevant. The law governing each event determines its effect.

Source: Chapter 1, printed pp. 34–36; Chapter 8, printed pp. 255–256, 275. Open Chapter 8.

Speaker notes

Ask students which object they are discussing whenever they say “the money”. Policy issue and company drawdown do not leave one unchanged asset throughout the transaction. The diagram summarises the book’s analysis and does not prescribe the effect of every possible instrument.

RECONSTRUCTING THE FILE

Keep three records distinct

RecordImmediate questionExample
Legal positionsWho holds which right or power?Policyholder, secured creditor, account holder
Value movementsWhat moved, when and through which route?Premium transfer, drawdown, repayment
Institutional informationWho holds, receives or may use the record?Insurer’s application file, bank mandate

A document may support several inquiries. Its evidential function must be stated for each one.

Source: Chapter 1, sections 1.3–1.6, printed pp. 35–41. Open the method.

Speaker notes

Use a premium receipt to illustrate the distinction. It may record a payment and help reconstruct value movement. It does not by itself establish a later proprietary entitlement or another institution’s knowledge. Invite students to name the precise proposition for which each document is offered.

CHAPTER 1

The completed opinion

  1. Reconstruct the transaction and its chronology.
  2. Determine the legal state of each component.
  3. State and test the connection asserted between components.
  4. Identify the operative legal basis, allocation rule and competent decision-maker.
  5. State the authorised consequence, remedy and reason to close or revisit the inquiry.

Source: Chapter 1, section 1.3, printed pp. 35–36. This is a teaching paraphrase of the book’s five-step method. Read section 1.3.

Speaker notes

The published method concerns the order in which a completed opinion makes its reasoning reviewable. Investigation itself can move backwards and forwards as facts and construction change the inquiry. A checklist of headings cannot supply the substantive law or missing evidence.

TESTING THE CONNECTION

Make the missing premise visible

“The policyholder supplied collateral, so the policyholder owns the company’s loan proceeds.”

Ask about the right

Which proprietary, contractual, trust, agency or other legal basis could support the asserted entitlement?

Ask about the proof

What do the facility, account mandate and relevant transfers establish? What evidence points the other way?

Source: Chapter 1, printed pp. 35–44; Chapter 5, printed p. 164; Chapter 8, printed p. 275. The quoted assertion is a fictional teaching example.

Speaker notes

Ask students to identify the inference before discussing whether it is persuasive. The company account is a distinct legal object. Collateral support may explain why credit was available, while ownership, authority to operate the account and benefit from the proceeds require separate foundations.

Fictional seminar percentages. A concern about one tranche does not establish the legal result for every other tranche.

Source: Chapter 2, printed pp. 58–59, 87–89. These teaching figures differ from the recurring transaction in the book. Read the tranche analysis.

Speaker notes

Ask what the bank’s processing decision proves. It records an operational gate opening on the information available. Truth of a representation, lawfulness of use, administrative consequence and downstream private-law effect still require their respective rules and evidence. The percentages do not create proportional beneficial ownership in the policy.

Speaker notes

The arithmetic is exact on the fictional inputs, but its legal use depends on assumptions. Ask whether the surrender amount is payable at the relevant date, whether other deductions or interests intervene, and who is entitled to the surplus. A marketing illustration or a lender’s estimate cannot answer all of those questions.

TRUST GOVERNANCE

Identify the property actually vested

Individual

Holds the policy and grants the security described in the instrument.

Trustee

Holds company shares in the capacity established by the transfer and deed.

Company

Holds its account asset and acts through its authorised organs.

Trustee-held shares do not themselves confer the individual’s power to surrender the policy.

Source: Chapter 4, printed pp. 129–130; Chapter 8, printed p. 275. The slide isolates the allocation relevant to the teaching example.

Speaker notes

Replace vague references to “the trust’s assets” with the specific property, transfer, holder and capacity. A trust governing-law clause may resolve internal questions while leaving external transfer, company, security and contractual questions to their own analysis. Ask students which instruments they would request.

RESERVED POWERS

Replacement leaves further decisions

Lee can replace the protector.

That affects who holds the office, subject to valid exercise of the power.

The proposed borrowing still needs

  • Protector consent under the deed
  • Relevant trustee shareholder action
  • Company board authority
  • Lender approval and account authority

Source: Chapter 5, printed pp. 163–164 and sections 5.6–5.9. Lee is a fictional classroom character.

Speaker notes

Trace the specific outcome rather than applying an undefined label of control. Replacement changes the office-holder’s identity. The powers, duties and independent decisions of each office remain to be analysed. A power to prevent a result may differ from a dependable ability to cause it.

CREDIT AND CONTROL

Prove each capacity separately

CapacityEvidence to investigate
Enable borrowingPolicy security, facility conditions and lender decision
Direct deploymentCorporate authority, instructions and relevant duties
Access proceedsAccount mandate and any other proved entitlement
Enjoy a benefitTransfers, contractual rights and the purpose-specific legal rule

Specify the actor, outcome and material date. Influence may be relevant evidence without resolving legal attribution.

Source: Chapter 5, printed pp. 163–164 and sections 5.8–5.9. Read the distinctions.

Speaker notes

Invite students to construct a case in which the security provider enables a company’s borrowing but has no right to use the proceeds. Then change one fact, such as an express contractual entitlement, and ask which conclusion can legitimately change. Keep the other conclusions intact unless the new fact reaches them.

STRUCTURAL LEGIBILITY

Can the authorised actor build the file?

The relevant capacity is to assemble, understand, test and correct the facts required for a specified decision.

Insurer

Application, policy and assignment records

Lender

Credit, collateral and release records

Trustee

Constitution, consent and administration records

For each transfer of information, identify authority to disclose, receive and use it.

Source: Chapter 6, printed pp. 193–195. Read Chapter 6.

Speaker notes

Ask students to name the recipient and decision before proposing more data sharing. A reporting field can carry a purpose-specific meaning. Accurate technical transmission does not make that field a conclusive answer to a different ownership or authority question. Privacy, confidence, privilege, verification and correction remain relevant.

DIAGNOSING THE FAILURE

The response depends on the gap

Substantive

The existing norm does not reach the relevant conduct.

Examine whether a new or revised rule is justified.

Evidential

The facts cannot be obtained, authenticated or tested.

Examine proof, production and verification.

Institutional

Records are fragmented without an assigned route to assemble the decision file.

Examine responsibility and lawful cooperation.

Several gaps may coexist. Check existing powers before claiming that a route is absent.

Source: Chapter 6, section 6.9; conclusion, printed pp. 280–282. Read the synthesis.

Speaker notes

Give students one fact pattern and ask them to propose three differently framed diagnoses. Require evidence for the chosen diagnosis. Broader liability cannot make a document authentic, and an information-sharing power cannot supply a substantive norm that has not been enacted.

Speaker notes

The slide teaches distinctions rather than making a current regulatory claim. A credential may establish practical signing capability while leaving legal authority unresolved. A licence does not prove that a product has launched or that the proposed customer can use it. Recheck current primary sources before real-world application.

SYNTHESIS

Keep the dates and surviving rights

At drawdown

Identify the borrower, debt, account asset, security and any proved rights of access.

After repayment and release

Identify which debt and security rights ended and which policy capacities were restored.

A later release does not rewrite ownership of earlier loan proceeds.

State the legal basis, claimant, defendant, remedy, surviving rights and event that would reopen an unresolved issue.

Source: Chapter 8, printed pp. 255–256, 275; conclusion, printed pp. 281–283. Read the historical synthesis.

Speaker notes

The recurring example distinguishes Month 36 from Month 48. Use those dates to explain that a current state and a historical finding can both be accurate. Ask students to specify the effect of the actual release instrument rather than assuming that every right has disappeared.

10-MINUTE CLASS EXERCISE · FICTIONAL FACTS

Write the next justified conclusion

The supplied file

  • One premium tranche has a documentary inconsistency.
  • The policy secures a company facility.
  • The account mandate excludes the policyholder.
  • A later release is documented.

Your 150-word opinion

  • State one established proposition.
  • Identify one unresolved issue and its missing evidence.
  • Reject or qualify one unsupported inference.
  • Name the event that would reopen your conclusion.

Original classroom exercise drawing on Chapters 1–2, 5 and 8. This invented file supplies no concluded finding of illegality.

Speaker notes

Suggested discussion: the mandate supports a conclusion about authorised account operation on the supplied record, while other possible entitlements remain separate. The premium inconsistency warrants a focused origin inquiry. It does not alone establish invalidity of the whole policy. The release changes the relevant security position according to its terms and does not settle historic ownership of proceeds. Accept alternative answers that identify their exact proposition, rule and evidential limit.
