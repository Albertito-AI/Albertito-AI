# IBOL teaching guide

Eight 90-minute seminars. Each lesson can also be used independently. The exercises are fictional and assess legal reasoning, not actual transaction legality.

# Reading a transaction across legal boundaries

Original teaching companion to Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity* (University of Groningen Press, 2026).

Chapter 1, printed pp. 29–53; 1.2–1.8. [Open the published PDF](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=31).

Legal cut-off: 1 August 2026. This lesson explains the edition; it does not verify subsequent law.

## Learning goals

- Separate legal positions, value movements and institutional information.
- Formulate a provable connection between two components.
- Identify the rule, decision-maker and remedy needed for a conclusion.

## Reading guide

Begin with the transaction chronology. A payment, an insurance contract, a security interest and a company account are distinct legal events or objects, even when they belong to one economic history. The chapter organises the legal opinion around reconstruction, component analysis, a tested relational proposition, the operative legal basis and the permitted consequence. Its evidence labels describe support for individual propositions. They do not replace a statutory burden of proof. The method also requires a stopping point: a connection can be contradicted, legally immaterial or unresolved on the available record.

## 90-minute seminar

- **10 minutes:** Recall the assigned reading and define the decision to be made.
- **20 minutes:** Discuss the reading guide and test its distinctions against the text.
- **25 minutes:** Work in pairs on the constructed exercise, recording missing evidence.
- **20 minutes:** Compare reasons with the facilitator commentary and challenge an inference.
- **10 minutes:** Debate the challenge question using a specified rule or evidence gap.
- **5 minutes:** Complete the exit task and identify a source to revisit.

## Constructed exercise

An individual funds a policy. Two years later that policy supports a company loan. An adviser concludes: “The individual therefore owns the loan proceeds.” The file contains a premium receipt, a security instrument and a company account statement. Prepare three short records: legal position, movement of value and information held. Then identify the missing premise in the adviser’s conclusion.

## Facilitator commentary

Payment and collateral support do not, by themselves, establish ownership of a later company account asset. Identify the policyholder, borrower and account holder separately. The proposed conclusion needs an applicable proprietary, contractual, agency, trust or other legal basis and evidence satisfying it. Ask what the security instrument transfers and what the account mandate permits. The answer may remain open if essential records are absent. A defensible opinion states the uncertainty and the record or legal event that could resolve it.

## Challenge for discussion

Could a rule-bound method protect sophisticated parties who control the evidence? Examine disclosure powers, burdens and justified inferences before deciding that the inquiry should close.

## Exit task

Write one relational proposition and state what evidence would disprove it.

# Tracing the origin of a premium

Original teaching companion to Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity* (University of Groningen Press, 2026).

Chapter 2, printed pp. 57–90; 2.1–2.4 and 2.10–2.12. [Open the published PDF](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=59).

Legal cut-off: 1 August 2026. This lesson explains the edition; it does not verify subsequent law.

## Learning goals

- Analyse each premium tranche by route and date.
- Distinguish an operational payment decision from a legal finding.
- Explain why a downstream consequence requires its own legal basis.

## Reading guide

The origin inquiry follows the value’s acquisition, currency conversion, remittance, declared purpose and actual use. Personal identity is insufficient to reconstruct that route. A single premium can combine legally different tranches, so the insurer’s aggregate receipt does not answer every origin question. The chapter also distinguishes the functions of legislation, administrative material, forms and bank processing. A successful transfer records an operational event on the information then supplied. It does not settle every issue of accuracy, lawfulness or later effect. Any Hong Kong consequence requires the relevant rule and procedure to be identified separately.

- **10 minutes:** Recall the assigned reading and define the decision to be made.
- **20 minutes:** Discuss the reading guide and test its distinctions against the text.
- **25 minutes:** Work in pairs on the constructed exercise, recording missing evidence.
- **20 minutes:** Compare reasons with the facilitator commentary and challenge an inference.
- **10 minutes:** Debate the challenge question using a specified rule or evidence gap.
- **5 minutes:** Complete the exit task and identify a source to revisit.

## Constructed exercise

A fictional premium is funded 50% from documented offshore earnings, 20% from pre-held foreign currency in a Mainland account and 30% from a new currency purchase. Only the final tranche has a purpose description apparently inconsistent with the insurer receipt. Design a document request and state three separate provisional findings. Do not assume the source percentages establish beneficial shares in the policy.

## Facilitator commentary

Request the source account history, acquisition record, relevant purchase or remittance form, supporting documents, bank communications and receipt for each tranche. Preserve the distinct routes. The inconsistent description is a reason for a focused inquiry, subject to clarification and the applicable rule. It does not establish a concluded breach or invalidate the whole policy. Identify the competent authority, relevant time and any cross-system route before stating a consequence. These percentages are invented for this seminar and differ from the book’s recurring example.

## Challenge for discussion

When is aggregation legally justified, and which additional facts and rule would support it?

## Exit task

Explain in 100 words what a payment-processing record proves and what remains unresolved.

# Policy rights, collateral and realisation

Original teaching companion to Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity* (University of Groningen Press, 2026).

Chapter 3, printed pp. 91–125; 3.1, 3.3 and 3.7–3.11. [Open the published PDF](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=93).

Legal cut-off: 1 August 2026. This lesson explains the edition; it does not verify subsequent law.

## Learning goals

- Distinguish premium, surrender value, event benefit and collateral value.
- Identify the right acquired under a security instrument.
- Analyse enforcement through claimant, priority, deductions and date.

## Reading guide

Policy issue creates contractual rights whose incidents depend on the policy and governing law. An economic contribution to the premium does not automatically allocate a proportional proprietary interest in those rights. The relevant value also changes with the question: a death benefit, present surrender entitlement, policy-loan capacity and lender’s recovery estimate concern different rights and assumptions. Assignment, notice, registration, priority and realisation require their own analysis. The chapter connects these matters without allowing a regulatory classification or a marketing illustration to determine private-law title.

## 90-minute seminar

- **10 minutes:** Recall the assigned reading and define the decision to be made.
- **20 minutes:** Discuss the reading guide and test its distinctions against the text.
- **25 minutes:** Work in pairs on the constructed exercise, recording missing evidence.
- **20 minutes:** Compare reasons with the facilitator commentary and challenge an inference.
- **10 minutes:** Debate the challenge question using a specified rule or evidence gap.
- **5 minutes:** Complete the exit task and identify a source to revisit.

## Constructed exercise

A fictional file lists a death benefit of 1,000,000 units, current surrender value of 620,000, secured debt of 310,000 and assumed realisation costs of 15,000. A lender’s internal collateral estimate is 500,000. Identify which numbers answer which questions. Calculate the purely illustrative residual using surrender value, debt and costs, then list the legal assumptions needed before anyone could claim it.

## Facilitator commentary

The arithmetic residual is 295,000 units. It is not an entitlement or recovery forecast. Its use assumes the stated surrender amount is currently payable, the debt and costs are correctly identified, no other deductions or competing interests intervene and the claimant has the relevant right. The death benefit concerns a different event. The lender’s estimate is a valuation assumption. Identify assignment terms, notice, priority, policy conditions and the person entitled to any surplus before reaching a legal conclusion.

## Challenge for discussion

How could a formally accurate valuation still mislead a client about accessible liquidity?

## Exit task

Produce a four-row table pairing each value with its claimant, event and source document.

# Identifying trust property and authority

Original teaching companion to Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity* (University of Groningen Press, 2026).

Chapter 4, printed pp. 129–161; 4.1–4.5 and 4.8–4.13. [Open the published PDF](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=131).

Legal cut-off: 1 August 2026. This lesson explains the edition; it does not verify subsequent law.

## Learning goals

- Identify the property actually vested in a trustee.
- Separate trust administration from company decisions.
- Match a proposed remedy to the relevant duty and asset.

## Reading guide

Start with the constitutive transfer and the property held. A trustee holding company shares occupies a different legal position from a trustee holding a policy or cash. The deed and trust law govern internal administration, while transfers, company acts, contracts and remedies may require other governing-law inquiries. The chapter treats limits on monitoring, delegation, consent and exemption as questions about specific duties and instruments. A broad label such as “family trust” conceals the offices and acts that must be proved.

- **10 minutes:** Recall the assigned reading and define the decision to be made.
- **20 minutes:** Discuss the reading guide and test its distinctions against the text.
- **25 minutes:** Work in pairs on the constructed exercise, recording missing evidence.
- **20 minutes:** Compare reasons with the facilitator commentary and challenge an inference.
- **10 minutes:** Debate the challenge question using a specified rule or evidence gap.
- **5 minutes:** Complete the exit task and identify a source to revisit.

## Constructed exercise

A fictional trustee holds shares in a borrower company. An individual retains the policy, which secures the company’s debt. A beneficiary asks the trustee to surrender the policy immediately. Draw the ownership relationships and identify the decision that the trustee can actually take on the supplied facts. List the instruments needed to investigate any further power.

## Facilitator commentary

Share ownership does not itself vest the company’s assets or the individual’s policy in the trustee. Begin with the share transfer, trust deed, company constitution, board authority, policy and security documents. The trustee may have shareholder powers subject to the relevant duties. That does not establish a right to surrender the policy. The proper claim, defendant and remedy depend on the property and duty actually engaged. The case deliberately leaves the content of further instruments unspecified.

## Challenge for discussion

Where should an adviser look when the trust deed appears permissive but the proposed act also requires company or lender consent?

## Exit task

Write a precise replacement for the statement “the insurance asset is in trust”.

# Powers, consent and practical control

Original teaching companion to Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity* (University of Groningen Press, 2026).

Chapter 5, printed pp. 163–190; 5.1–5.2 and 5.6–5.11. [Open the published PDF](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=165).

Legal cut-off: 1 August 2026. This lesson explains the edition; it does not verify subsequent law.

## Learning goals

- Distinguish possession, exercisability and exercise of a power.
- Identify independent decisions between influence and an outcome.
- Separate collateral support from access to loan proceeds.

## Reading guide

The relevant question is what a particular actor can lawfully achieve at a specified date. A power to replace an office-holder may affect who decides, while leaving the decision and its duties with that office. Consent, board approval, lender discretion and account authority may each remain independent requirements. The chapter also distinguishes enabling another person’s borrowing from directing, accessing or enjoying the proceeds. Each capacity needs a separate legal and evidential foundation.

## 90-minute seminar

- **10 minutes:** Recall the assigned reading and define the decision to be made.
- **20 minutes:** Discuss the reading guide and test its distinctions against the text.
- **25 minutes:** Work in pairs on the constructed exercise, recording missing evidence.
- **20 minutes:** Compare reasons with the facilitator commentary and challenge an inference.
- **10 minutes:** Debate the challenge question using a specified rule or evidence gap.
- **5 minutes:** Complete the exit task and identify a source to revisit.

## Constructed exercise

In a fictional deed, Lee may replace the protector. A proposed borrowing needs protector consent, trustee shareholder action, board approval and lender approval. The company’s bank mandate names two directors and excludes Lee. Lee says: “I can make the company borrow and spend the proceeds.” Identify what the stated documents establish, contradict and leave open.

## Facilitator commentary

The replacement power establishes a limited power concerning the office-holder’s identity, subject to its valid exercise. It does not itself supply consent, corporate authority or lender approval. The mandate contradicts an assertion that Lee is an authorised account signatory on this record. Other rights or facts might matter, but none are supplied. Distinguish a possible ability to prevent an outcome from a dependable legal route to cause it. An evidential account of influence must remain distinct from a concluded attribution of control.

## Challenge for discussion

Can a veto amount to control for one legal purpose but be insufficient for another? Specify the purpose before answering.

## Exit task

Identify the next independent decision that could interrupt Lee’s proposed route.

# Who can assemble the decision file?

Original teaching companion to Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity* (University of Groningen Press, 2026).

Chapter 6, printed pp. 193–226; 6.1–6.3 and 6.8–6.11. [Open the published PDF](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=195).

Legal cut-off: 1 August 2026. This lesson explains the edition; it does not verify subsequent law.

## Learning goals

- Define structural legibility for a specified decision.
- Distinguish substantive, evidential and institutional gaps.
- Identify lawful collection, disclosure, receipt and use.

## Reading guide

A complete factual picture is rarely held by one institution. The book asks whether an authorised decision-maker can lawfully assemble, test and correct the facts needed for its decision. This is more specific than a general demand for transparency. A reporting classification has a purpose within its own regime. Its transmission does not automatically make it proof of ownership or authority for another purpose. Reform depends on diagnosis: harmful conduct beyond the adequate reach of existing substantive rules, facts that cannot be proved and fragmented institutional responsibility call for different responses. The substantive-gap diagnosis requires a defensible account of why the uncovered conduct should be regulated.

## 90-minute seminar

- **10 minutes:** Recall the assigned reading and define the decision to be made.
- **20 minutes:** Discuss the reading guide and test its distinctions against the text.
- **25 minutes:** Work in pairs on the constructed exercise, recording missing evidence.
- **20 minutes:** Compare reasons with the facilitator commentary and challenge an inference.
- **10 minutes:** Debate the challenge question using a specified rule or evidence gap.
- **5 minutes:** Complete the exit task and identify a source to revisit.

## Constructed exercise

An insurer holds policy records, a lender holds the credit file and a trustee holds consent documents. Each has met its stated retention duty. A later decision requires all three records, but the case supplies no entitlement to compel or combine them. Diagnose the possible gap and design a narrowly framed information route. Include correction and contestability.

## Facilitator commentary

The facts suggest a possible institutional allocation problem, subject to checking existing production, cooperation and disclosure routes. State who needs which facts for what decision. Identify the sender’s authority to disclose, the recipient’s authority to receive and use, verification requirements and safeguards for confidentiality, privilege and personal data. An evidential problem may coexist if the records cannot be authenticated. No general right to combine all data follows merely from its usefulness.

## Challenge for discussion

Could greater information sharing make a decision less reliable if a field loses its original legal meaning?

## Exit task

Describe one reform in terms of actor, information, purpose, correction and lawful authority.

# Legal status and digital transactions

Original teaching companion to Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity* (University of Groningen Press, 2026).

Chapter 7, printed pp. 227–252; 7.1–7.5 and 7.9–7.12. [Open the published PDF](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=229).

Legal cut-off: 1 August 2026. This lesson explains the edition; it does not verify subsequent law.

## Learning goals

- Separate enactment, commencement, guidance and operational availability.
- Distinguish technical signing ability from legal authority.
- Identify the obligations and risks introduced by a digital intermediary.

## Reading guide

Future-facing advice begins with the status and date of each source. A Bill, enacted text awaiting commencement and a rule in force support different propositions. A licence does not prove that a particular product has launched or that a particular customer can use it. Digital form introduces questions about issuer obligations, custody, credentials, payment discharge and failure. The chapter applies these distinctions to proposed transactions without rewriting an earlier completed legal history. Its discussion is fixed to the edition’s legal cut-off and requires fresh primary-source checking for present advice.

## 90-minute seminar

- **10 minutes:** Recall the assigned reading and define the decision to be made.
- **20 minutes:** Discuss the reading guide and test its distinctions against the text.
- **25 minutes:** Work in pairs on the constructed exercise, recording missing evidence.
- **20 minutes:** Compare reasons with the facilitator commentary and challenge an inference.
- **10 minutes:** Debate the challenge question using a specified rule or evidence gap.
- **5 minutes:** Complete the exit task and identify a source to revisit.

## Constructed exercise

A fictional proposal would pay a supplier using a digital unit. The file contains a press release, an enacted but uncommenced provision, a custodian’s terms and evidence that an employee controls the signing key. The supplier’s acceptance terms are missing. Prepare a status table and identify the questions that prevent a conclusion that payment would discharge the invoice.

## Facilitator commentary

Record each source’s issuer, date, legal status and proposition. The press release may describe an intention; the uncommenced provision does not establish a present duty solely by enactment. Examine the unit’s claim, issuer, custody arrangement, corporate mandate and supplier agreement. Key possession proves practical signing capability, subject to the evidence, rather than authority or discharge. Any conclusion on current regulation needs current primary sources. The exercise gives no rule authorising the proposed payment.

## Challenge for discussion

What evidence should reopen a previous opinion when a scheme launches or a provision commences?

## Exit task

List three separate propositions hidden in the phrase “the transaction is approved”.

# Writing an opinion that can be challenged

Original teaching companion to Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity* (University of Groningen Press, 2026).

Chapter 8, printed pp. 255–277; 8.1–8.8; conclusion pp. 279–283. [Open the published PDF](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=257).

Legal cut-off: 1 August 2026. This lesson explains the edition; it does not verify subsequent law.

## Learning goals

- Preserve distinct component findings in a final synthesis.
- Match each consequence to a claimant, actor, rule and remedy.
- State surviving rights, limits and review triggers.

## Reading guide

Synthesis collects the completed findings without turning economic continuity into a single asset or a transaction-wide legal verdict. Policy issue, drawdown and release create or end different rights at different dates. A historical connection can remain relevant even after current borrowing capacity ends, but its legal significance still depends on the rule invoked. The final opinion should identify what was established, which gateway permits a consequence, whose rights are affected and which rights survive. A conclusion can also remain bounded by missing evidence or a legal limit.

## 90-minute seminar

- **10 minutes:** Recall the assigned reading and define the decision to be made.
- **20 minutes:** Discuss the reading guide and test its distinctions against the text.
- **25 minutes:** Work in pairs on the constructed exercise, recording missing evidence.
- **20 minutes:** Compare reasons with the facilitator commentary and challenge an inference.
- **10 minutes:** Debate the challenge question using a specified rule or evidence gap.
- **5 minutes:** Complete the exit task and identify a source to revisit.

## Constructed exercise

A fictional opinion records a documentary inconsistency in one premium tranche, an effective policy assignment, company ownership of the loan account and no proved policyholder access. At a later review, repayment and release are documented. Write a 250-word opinion separating the historic findings from the current position and identify one event that would reopen each unresolved issue.

## Facilitator commentary

Keep the provenance concern distinct from the policy, security and account findings. The historic drawdown created the company’s debt and account asset on the supplied facts. Later repayment and release change the debt and security position to the extent proved by the instruments; they do not establish who owned earlier proceeds. Avoid a single verdict on “the structure”. State the scope of each conclusion, the decision-maker, the available or unproved remedy, surviving rights and the missing record or legal change that warrants review.

## Challenge for discussion

When does closing an inquiry reflect a sound legal limit, and when does it conceal an avoidable failure to obtain evidence?

## Exit task

Exchange opinions with a partner and identify one unsupported inference and one adequately bounded conclusion.
