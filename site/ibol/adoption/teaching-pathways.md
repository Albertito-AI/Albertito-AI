# Teaching pathways for Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity

A practical adoption guide for Albert Tan Sin Hon, *Insurance-Backed Offshore Liquidity: Financial Law, Trust Governance and Regulatory Visibility at the PRC-Hong Kong Interface* (University of Groningen Press, 2026), DOI [10.21827/6ac3948159a9e](https://doi.org/10.21827/6ac3948159a9e).

[Read the open-access book](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/book/300) · [Open the learning companion](../index.html) · [Browse the existing seminars](../teaching-guide.md)

The book is intended chiefly for advanced postgraduate readers and specialists. Teachers can use the introductory session below to prepare students who have some familiarity with legal reasoning but have not studied insurance finance. The session introduces the book's method; it is not a complete course in the substantive law.

All page references below are **printed book pages**. The linked publisher PDF has two preceding physical pages, so printed page 37 opens at PDF page 39. The legal cut-off is 1 August 2026. These materials explain that edition and make no claim to verify subsequent legal developments.

## A 45-minute introduction: what does the document prove?

**Preparation:** read printed p. 37 (the three working records), pp. 40–42 (propositions and evidence), and pp. 48–49 (legal basis and consequence). These are six selected pages. Students may use an accessible text reader or inspect the PDF. Teachers should explain unfamiliar vocabulary before the exercise.

**Learning outcomes:** students should be able to separate an economic payment from a legal entitlement; record what a named institution actually holds; formulate one proposition with supporting and contrary evidence; and identify the missing legal premise before suggesting a remedy.

| Time | Activity | Student output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 minutes | Ask students what a payment receipt establishes and what it leaves unresolved. | One supported fact and one unanswered question. |
| 5–13 minutes | Explain the three working records with reference to printed p. 37. Distinguish a document's contents from the truth or legal effect of those contents. | A page divided into legal position, value movement and institutional information. |
| 13–25 minutes | In pairs, examine the fictional file below. Attach each entry to its document and date. | A short three-record reconstruction, with uncertain entries marked. |
| 25–37 minutes | Exchange files. Test the proposed inference and identify the source of law or contractual term needed to assess it. | One precise proposition, its evidential status and a potential counterexample. |
| 37–45 minutes | Discuss why an information gap can leave an opinion unresolved. Complete the exit task. | A bounded conclusion with the next document or legal question specified. |

### Fictional classroom file

Mira pays a premium for an insurance policy. The policy schedule names Cedar Ltd as policyholder. Eighteen months later a lender receives an assignment document describing the policy as collateral for Cedar Ltd's borrowing. A bank statement records the loan proceeds in Cedar Ltd's account. The lender's file contains the schedule and assignment but no record explaining why Mira paid the premium.

A colleague proposes: “Mira paid for the policy, so she can require the lender to transfer the loan proceeds to her.” No trust instrument, reimbursement agreement, account mandate, insurer acknowledgement or governing-law analysis is supplied. These are invented facts for discussion and do not describe an actual transaction.

**Pair task:** record the payment, the named policyholder, the proposed assignment, the borrowing and the lender's information separately. Decide what each supplied document can support. Then rewrite the colleague's statement as a proposition that could be tested under an identified legal basis. State which missing record could support or contradict it.

### Facilitator notes

The receipt can support the fact of payment, subject to provenance and authenticity. It does not establish every right that might follow from the payment. The schedule supplies evidence of the named policyholder; the assignment requires construction and attention to any relevant formalities. The statement supports a credit to the company account. Each proposition still needs its own evidential and legal assessment.

Students should recognise possible questions about reimbursement, a trust or another proprietary basis without inventing an agreement or deciding which doctrine governs. The given file does not establish Mira's alleged power to compel the lender. A satisfactory answer explains the gap and identifies an investigation capable of resolving it. A stronger answer considers evidence that could defeat the proposed entitlement and identifies who could lawfully obtain or use that evidence.

Use the book's evidence labels for individual propositions. Do not award a higher mark simply because a student writes “established” or supplies a more confident conclusion. The labels describe support in the record; statutory burdens and standards of proof retain their own operation.

**Exit task:** in no more than 100 words, identify one document-supported fact, one unresolved legal premise and one justified next step. Invite students to revise their answer after reading the full first chapter.

## A four-week focused pathway

This route suits a postgraduate reading group or a short unit within financial law, trusts or legal method. It selects about 19–22 printed pages each week. It does not cover every doctrine in the book. Allow one 90-minute class and an initial planning estimate of two to three hours for reading and a short preparation note each week; adjust that estimate to students' language needs and prior knowledge. A final 600-word opinion may require a further one to two hours.

**Suggested class rhythm:** 10 minutes for the reading question, 20 minutes for close discussion, 30 minutes for one principal exercise, 20 minutes for peer challenge and 10 minutes for revision. This totals 90 minutes. The linked full seminars are alternative resources; combining every linked activity would exceed the allotted time.

**Final task:** submit a 600-word opinion on one of the companion's fictional files, accompanied by a one-page document-and-proposition schedule. State the scope of the opinion, the material date, two supported findings, one serious counterargument and one unresolved matter. Distinguish a request for further evidence from a concluded adverse legal finding. Use book section and page references; any claim about present law requires separate current primary-source checking.

**Teaching choices:** assess the quality of the reasoning and the fit between evidence and conclusion. A carefully justified unresolved conclusion may be stronger than a confident result. Students needing a narrower introduction can begin with the 45-minute session above. Those pursuing insurance security, trust powers or digital transactions in depth should take the longer course pathway or read the relevant complete chapters.

## An eight-week postgraduate course

This route assigns one complete chapter per week and uses the eight existing 90-minute seminars. It provides 12 contact hours and 238 pages of chapter reading, excluding the prefatory material, introduction, conclusion and appendices. Plan initially for three to five hours of preparation each week, including reading, checking an assigned authority and producing a one-page working note. These are planning estimates. Teachers should reduce the reading or extend the course where students need additional support in trusts, insurance, secured finance or cross-system legal analysis.

Before the first class, invite students to read the book's guide to use and preliminary note, printed pp. 6–9. Make clear that a taught course can assess the book's argument while challenging its method or conclusions. The constructed files supply examples for doctrinal reasoning; they do not establish market prevalence or participants' motives.

**Assessment sequence:** use the weekly notes for formative feedback. After week 4, review a 400-word provisional opinion and one-page evidence schedule. After week 8, request a 1,500-word final opinion, with the revised schedule and a 200-word explanation of what changed. Teachers may replace the final opinion with an oral defence where that better tests the intended learning outcomes. Give the final assessment its own preparation allowance rather than treating it as included in the weekly reading estimate.

**Authority checking:** allocate one short primary-source passage per student or pair each week. Ask them to identify the source, its status, the proposition for which the book uses it and any relevant limitation. This task supports critical reading; it should not become an unsupported claim that the class has verified the whole book. Where a source cannot be obtained, the note should identify the access problem and its consequence for the proposed conclusion.

**Optional closing reading:** the conclusion, [pp. 279–283](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=281), adds five pages. Ask students whether the coordinating method changes legal analysis materially or mostly makes existing professional practice explicit. Require a concrete example and a serious objection. An extension on present law should be separately labelled and checked against current primary sources rather than silently extending the book's legal cut-off.

## An adaptable assessment rubric

This proposed rubric can support the short opinion or the longer course assessment. It is original teaching guidance, not a rule prescribed by the book, publisher or any institution. Teachers should publish the chosen weighting and marking conventions before assessment and align them with their institution's requirements.

| Criterion | Suggested weight | Strong performance | Developing performance | Material weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal basis and institutional competence | 25% | Identifies the relevant legal question, source and status, actor, material date, decision-maker and available consequence. Explains the limits of the proposed legal route. | Identifies a plausible legal route but leaves an element, capacity, source status or procedural step insufficiently explained. | Derives a legal consequence from the transaction's label, economic connection or perceived unfairness without an applicable legal basis. |
| Evidence and source use | 25% | Links each material factual proposition to an identifiable source; distinguishes the document's contents, authentication, inference and legal effect; addresses contrary material. | Uses relevant sources but leaves provenance, the inferential step or counter-evidence underdeveloped. | Invents a fact or source, treats missing material as proof, or uses a source for a proposition it cannot support. |
| Application and counterargument | 20% | Applies the identified rule to the specified facts and capacities; explains a serious competing interpretation and why it succeeds, fails or remains open. | Reaches a plausible conclusion but gives limited attention to an alternative explanation or a change of fact that would matter. | Repeats general doctrine or a preferred outcome without connecting the rule and evidence to the particular issue. |
| Uncertainty, scope and review | 20% | States precisely what is established, unresolved or contradicted; confines the opinion to its purpose and date; identifies a lawful next step or event requiring review. | Recognises uncertainty but does not show its consequence for the disposition or identify a focused next step. | Gives unwarranted certainty, applies one evidence label to the whole file, or treats every gap as a reason to stop without considering available lawful inquiry. |
| Clarity and reproducibility | 10% | Uses precise terms and sufficient citations so another reader can trace the reasoning. Separates historic findings, current position and suggested further work. | Is generally intelligible but leaves some terms, dates or citations ambiguous. | Makes the reasoning difficult to reconstruct through unclear attribution, unsupported shorthand or missing source locations. |

**One workable scoring method:** assess each criterion on a 0–4 scale, where 4 means consistently demonstrated, 3 substantially demonstrated with limited gaps, 2 partly demonstrated, 1 demonstrated only weakly, and 0 absent. Multiply the criterion's weighting by the score divided by four, then add the five results. This yields a percentage; any institutional grade conversion remains for the teacher. Record a short reason and one piece of student work supporting each score.

For example, scores of 3, 3, 2, 4 and 3 yield 18.75 + 18.75 + 10 + 20 + 7.5 = **75%**. The numerical example illustrates calculation only; it does not set a pass mark or imply that this pattern has been observed in a student submission.

**Moderation:** before marking the class, compare two anonymised or invented sample answers and discuss material disagreements. Reward defensible reasoning even where the conclusion differs from the facilitator's. A student who explains why the supplied file cannot establish an entitlement can perform strongly; a student who identifies a valid alternative legal route can also do so. Confidence alone carries no credit. Do not count a single citation error repeatedly under every criterion unless it causes distinct analytical defects.

**Feedback prompts:** identify the strongest supported proposition; identify the conclusion that most needs another premise; and specify one revision that would improve the opinion. Where permitted tools or assistance are relevant, state the course's attribution requirements in advance and assess the student's demonstrated analysis under those requirements. This guide supplies no separate academic-integrity procedure.

The rubric takes its analytical aims from the book's treatment of [proposition-specific evidence, printed pp. 40–44](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=42), [operative legal basis and consequence, printed pp. 48–49](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=50), and the [bounded preliminary conclusion, printed p. 53](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=55). The proposed weights and scoring method are separate pedagogical choices.

## Evidence-file assignment: a claim to the company's proceeds

This is a self-contained fictional file, separate from the earlier Mira/Cedar exercise and from the book's recurring transaction. Every person, organisation, document and amount below is invented. The exercise tests reasoning about the edition; it supplies no conclusion about an actual transaction.

**Retainer:** at a fictional review date of 1 August 2026, an adviser must assess Ari's claim that paying for an insurance policy and holding a power to replace a protector entitles Ari to direct money borrowed by Juniper Ltd. The supplied materials are abbreviated document descriptions. Treat the copies as authentic for classroom purposes, but do not treat authenticity as proving every assertion, the effectiveness of an instrument or the completeness of the file. All amounts are in fictional currency units.

| File item | Supplied record | Evidential question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1, 7 January | Insurer receipt recording an 80,000 premium payment by Ari for policy P4. | What does receipt establish about payment, and what does it leave open about source and entitlement? |
| D2, 8 January, with a valuation dated 1 June | Policy schedule naming Ari as policyholder. The later valuation states a surrender figure of 52,000, subject to the policy conditions. Full policy terms are absent. | Which object and named party are identified? What further conditions govern access to the stated value? |
| D3, 1 June | Signed security document describing P4 as collateral for a loan to Juniper Ltd. Only the signature page and the description of the collateral are supplied. | Can the scope, formal effectiveness and priority of the lender's interest be resolved from these pages? |
| D4, 2 June | Insurer message acknowledging receipt of the security document and stating that verification is pending. | Does receipt of the document establish acceptance, completion of a formality or priority? Which additional rule or term would answer that question? |
| D5, 5 June | Statement crediting 30,000 loan proceeds to an account in Juniper Ltd's name. The supplied account mandate names two directors as joint signatories and does not name Ari. | What does the statement show about receipt? What does the mandate support or contradict about Ari's signatory authority? |
| D6, undated extract | Trust-deed extract describing Elm Trustees as intended trustee of Juniper Ltd shares. It requires protector consent for a specified borrowing decision above 25,000 and gives Ari a power to replace the protector. Neither the completed share transfer nor the full deed is supplied. | What remains to be proved about the property constituted, the scope of the consent requirement and the replacement power? |
| D7, 3 June | Lender committee note approving the proposed facility subject to receipt of the board resolution and written protector consent. A consent form is present but unsigned; the board resolution is absent. | Does the file show that the conditions were fulfilled, waived or handled under another provision? |
| D8, 10 June | Ari's email: “I funded the policy and can replace the protector, so I can tell the company how to spend the loan.” No instruction to the bank or subsequent payment is supplied. | Which part is an assertion of entitlement, and what would be needed to support it? |

**Student submission:** provide an 800-word provisional opinion and a one-page schedule containing no more than eight material propositions. In the schedule, identify the source, its date, what it supports, contrary material, any missing premise and the book's evidence label. Explain the legal basis that would be required for Ari's asserted entitlement, identify three proportionate document requests, and state what the adviser can responsibly conclude now. Identify the institution or person whose authority would be required to obtain any missing record. Do not assume a general entitlement to another person's file.

**Suggested allocation:** 20 minutes to reconstruct the records, 25 minutes to build the proposition schedule, 25 minutes to draft the opinion and 20 minutes to peer-review it. The 90-minute workshop produces a draft; allow further time for a polished assessed submission and authority checking.

### Facilitator outline

- Begin with the identity of each object and actor. D1 concerns a payment. D2 identifies policy rights and an indicative contractual value. D3–D4 concern a proposed or purported security relation whose full legal effect requires more material. D5 concerns the company account and its mandate. D6 concerns an intended trust shareholding and specified offices.
- The missing transfer and full deed qualify any conclusion that the shares were constituted as trust property. Even an established trust shareholding would require a separate analysis of company assets and company decision-making.
- D5 supports a credit to the company's account and does not identify Ari as a signatory under the supplied mandate. It does not exhaust every possible authority, proprietary claim or arrangement. Students should identify a proposed alternative only if they also state the legal and evidential premise needed to sustain it.
- D6 may support a replacement power on the described terms. Its lawful exercise, successor's assumption of office, protector consent, company decision and account authority remain separate questions. D8 is evidence that Ari asserted a position, not proof that the assertion is legally correct.
- D7 leaves the completion or treatment of conditions unresolved. Drawdown does not establish which documents were received, whether a condition was waived or what consequence a defect would have. The unsigned form is a reason for inquiry, not a complete legal conclusion about the facility.
- Suitable requests include the full policy and security terms with notice records; the complete deed, constitution and share-transfer documents; and the actual board resolution, consent and lender completion record. Prioritisation should follow the question being decided and an available lawful route.

A well-reasoned provisional conclusion states that the supplied file does not establish Ari's claimed entitlement. It leaves open a properly supported alternative legal basis and explains what could resolve it. The exercise supplies no factual basis for a concluded foreign-exchange breach, dishonesty or transaction-wide invalidity. Mark the identification and treatment of missing premises, rather than the number of legal labels listed.

**Source anchors:** [printed p. 37](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=39), three working records; [pp. 40–44](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=42), evidence and relational propositions; [p. 138](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=140), identification of trust property; [pp. 164–168](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=166), power, office and present exercisability; [pp. 193–199](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=195), purpose-specific information operations.

## Oral examination: defend a bounded opinion

A 15-minute oral defence can test whether the student can explain the relationship between a source, a factual proposition and a conclusion. It is a proposed assessment format, subject to the course's published rules. Use the same core file and comparable question difficulty across students. Provide the criteria, permitted materials and arrangements for appropriate adjustments in advance.

**Preparation:** assign the fictional Ari/Juniper file above and allow a one-page proposition schedule with source references. For a short formative version, allow 20 minutes of preparation immediately before the discussion. For a summative examination, state the preparation arrangements in the assessment instructions. The exercise assesses legal reasoning and source use, not memorisation of page numbers.

| Time | Examiner's task | Evidence to listen for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 minutes | Ask for the student's provisional conclusion and the question it answers. | A defined issue, material date and bounded disposition rather than a general verdict on the structure. |
| 3–7 minutes | Select one material document and ask what it proves, what it does not prove and which legal source is needed. | Source-specific reasoning, the distinction between authenticity and truth, and an identified legal premise. |
| 7–11 minutes | Introduce one disclosed variation and invite a revised conclusion. | Recognition of what changes, what survives and which additional premise remains necessary. |
| 11–14 minutes | Ask for the strongest objection and a justified next step. | A serious competing account, proportionate inquiry and attention to authority for obtaining or using the missing material. |
| 14–15 minutes | Invite a final qualification or correction. | A concise revision that improves the answer; students should receive credit for a reasoned correction. |

### Question bank and examiner guidance

| Prompt | A defensible response should address |
| --- | --- |
| “D1 records Ari's payment. What further premise would establish the entitlement claimed in D8?” | An applicable contractual, proprietary or other legal basis and evidence meeting its conditions. Payment alone leaves the alleged power over company proceeds unproved. |
| “What follows from D4's acknowledgement?” | The document records receipt and pending verification. Any further effect depends on the instrument, relevant formality and governing law. |
| “D5 does not name Ari as a signatory. Does that conclude the whole dispute?” | It confines the supplied mandate evidence. Another authority or claim would need its own source and proof; it should neither be assumed nor excluded without analysis. |
| “What difference does a complete share-transfer record make to D6?” | It may resolve a constitution issue, subject to the applicable law. It does not by itself place the company's account assets or Ari's policy into the trust. |
| “What is the strongest answer to Ari's replacement-power argument?” | Replacement changes an office-holder through a valid process. Consent, company action and account authority remain distinct. A student may investigate influence or another control rule if the required legal purpose and facts are stated. |
| “The lender now produces a signed consent. What changes?” | The particular evidence gap may narrow. The signatory's authority, proper exercise, other conditions and the scope of Ari's alleged entitlement still require analysis. |
| “A later mandate names Ari as an authorised signatory. What can now be concluded?” | Its authenticity, effective date, terms and corporate authority must be assessed. Valid account authority would address a defined capacity; beneficial ownership and unrestricted personal use are separate questions. |
| “Which missing item should be sought first, and through what route?” | A justified priority linked to the question and the likely custodian, with an identified lawful authority or an expressly unresolved access issue. |

These are prompts for reasoning, not a compulsory script or a list of expected phrases. Use only one variation in a short examination. State the variation completely and give the student time to distinguish new stipulated facts from matters still open. Do not penalise a student for declining to invent an absent term or rule.

**Marking:** apply the published rubric above to the reasoning actually demonstrated. Make brief notes of the proposition or answer supporting each criterion score. An articulate but unsupported conclusion should not outrank a carefully supported, qualified answer. Accent, speed and polished presentation should not substitute for the legal outcomes being assessed. Where oral communication is itself an intended outcome, define and assess it expressly under the course's own criteria.

**Consistency:** use a common opening question and a comparable variation for each student, record substantive follow-up questions and moderate a sample of marking decisions. Explain material score differences by reference to evidence in the answer. A supported correction during the final minute can demonstrate learning and sound judgment. Apply the institution's published arrangements for alternative formats, record retention, review and appeals.

**Source basis:** the examination applies the book's [evidence schedule, printed pp. 40–44](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=42), [power and office analysis, printed pp. 164–168](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=166), and [bounded synthesis, printed pp. 265–268](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=267). Timing, adjustments, question selection and marking arrangements are original pedagogical proposals.

## A 60-minute critical journal club

**Question:** does relational legal analysis improve the quality of an opinion, and can its discipline of legal form adequately address unequal control of evidence?

Assign 16 printed pages: [pp. 30–35](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=32), [pp. 193–194](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=195), [pp. 217–219](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=219) and [pp. 279–283](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=281). Ask each participant to bring one claim the reading supports, one objection and one passage that could answer the objection. The discussion concerns the book's argument; it does not stand in for reading every source with which the author engages.

| Time | Discussion task | Record to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 minutes | State the method and its claimed contribution fairly. | A two-sentence account the other participants accept as accurate. |
| 5–15 minutes | Reconstruct one example of a missing inferential or legal premise. | The two components, the asserted connection and the rule said to make it material. |
| 15–30 minutes | Test the strongest originality or practical-use objection. | One concrete way the method improves, duplicates or burdens existing practice. |
| 30–45 minutes | Debate unequal access to evidence and the proposed access audit. | A specific residual problem after available lawful procedures have been considered. |
| 45–55 minutes | Evaluate a reform or a research design that could test the claim. | Its intended outcome, evidence needed and principal limitation. |
| 55–60 minutes | Revise the opening account and record remaining disagreement. | One supported judgment and one question left open. |

### Critical questions

- **Contribution:** the conclusion acknowledges that the individual techniques are familiar and claims value for their coordinated use. What analytical error does that coordination prevent in a specified file? Would a competent conventional legal opinion already avoid it? Identify the passage and the practical comparison needed to answer.
- **Proportionality:** when does maintaining separate records improve reasoning, and when does it impose work disproportionate to the issue? The book rejects completing every analytic dimension indiscriminately, at printed p. 40. Can a teacher or practitioner formulate a workable rule for selecting the relevant records?
- **Unequal proof:** the book's response to the distributional objection includes an access audit, available production routes and justified inference. After those measures are considered, could lawful cost, delay or institutional weakness still protect the better-resourced party? Specify what further reform would be needed and which competing interest it would affect.
- **Gap diagnosis:** classifying a substantive gap requires a defensible account of conduct that should be regulated. Who supplies that normative judgment? Could participants agree on the factual record and still reasonably disagree whether the law has a gap? Explain how that disagreement would alter the proposed reform.
- **Institutional choice:** structural legibility is relative to a decision and an authorised actor. What happens if no existing actor has responsibility for the harm the critic identifies? Distinguish the assessment of current powers from a proposal to allocate new responsibility.
- **Comparative limits:** which parts of the method travel across legal systems, and which depend on local concepts of title, evidence, privilege or authority? Use a specific issue rather than treating the method's vocabulary as universally decisive.
- **Empirical extension:** the constructed files do not establish prevalence, motive or typicality. What evidence would be needed to assess whether the method improves professional performance? One possible study would compare appropriately matched analyses of disclosed files, with independent scoring for unsupported inference, overlooked issues, time and reproducibility. Discuss selection effects, expertise and the difficulty of defining the correct answer before treating such a design as adequate.

**Facilitator approach:** invite one participant to give the strongest defence and another the strongest criticism, then exchange roles. Keep the author’s stated response in view before deciding that an objection is unanswered. In particular, do not attribute to the book an unconditional instruction to close a file whenever evidence is missing: printed pp. 218–219 expressly require inquiry into access and procedural possibilities. Equally, the existence of that requirement does not establish that it will cure practical inequality.

**Closing output:** write a 200-word appraisal distinguishing a claim established by the argument, a claim requiring further support and a proposed improvement. Cite at least two precise passages. Agreement with the author is not an assessment criterion; fair reconstruction and supported evaluation are.

## A compact learner glossary

These short explanations help readers return to the book's fuller analysis. They summarise the method in original wording and do not replace the governing doctrine. Page references identify the published edition.

| Legal object | The particular asset, claim, debt, power or record whose legal position is being examined. Naming an economic flow does not identify every object created along it. | [p. 33, Table 1.1](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=35) |
| Juridical hinge | An act or event through which a legal position changes, such as issue, assignment, drawdown or release. Its effect depends on the relevant instrument and law. | [pp. 33–34](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=35) |
| Juridical interface | A proved connection through which one component becomes relevant to another, such as a security assignment or an authorised information route. | Structural legibility | Whether an authorised actor can lawfully and in time assemble, understand, test and correct the facts needed for a specified decision. | Gap diagnosis | Ask whether the problem lies in the substantive rule, the obtainable proof or the allocation of institutional responsibility. **Evidence labels:** at [printed p. 41, Table 1.5 and accompanying text](https://books.ugp.rug.nl/ugp/catalog/download/300/584/1875#page=43), *established* indicates reliable primary support without a material unresolved contradiction; *strongly supported* leaves an important verification step; *open* preserves a necessary gap or responsible competing explanation; *contradicted* records reliable opposing evidence; and *not assessable* identifies an unavailable necessary source or standard, or a matter beyond the defined retainer. Attach each label to a precise proposition. These professional descriptions do not replace statutory burdens, presumptions or standards of proof.
