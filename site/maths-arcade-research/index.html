Teach with the six games

Ask each player to predict a move, explain the choice and try the idea on a different position. The Maths Arcade teaching collection covers Sudoku, Lattice Dash, Target Forge, Parity Lights, Nim Lab and Reversi Endgame Lab.

Atlas Sudoku

Every row, column and three-by-three box must contain each digit from 1 to 9 exactly once. The same rule can force one entry or certify a whole grid.

Try Warm-up, seed 0. The first row reads . 8 . 5 . 4 . . 1. Row 1 still needs 2, 3, 6, 7 and 9. Column 3 already contains 3, 6, 7 and 9. The upper-left box permits 2, so row 1, column 3 must be 2.

A naked single is a cell with one possible digit. A hidden single is a digit with one possible location in a row, column or box. Warm-up puzzles yield to naked singles. Tricky puzzles require at least one hidden single under the game's solving order. Every forced move agrees with every possible completion, so a sequence that fills the grid proves uniqueness. For Expert, the generator checks that there is exactly one completion and that the two single techniques stall.

The seed in a shared link lets another player inspect the same opening in the documented release. Fresh-play history reduces recent repeats on the device. The set of possible boards is finite.

Boardroom Bonus

A board wants to minimise emissions. Its manager earns a bonus for reducing emissions from the firm's own operations. Which investment wins under each rule?

Fictional emissions units for the same output and period
PositionOwnSupplierTotal
Baseline8020100
A · Upgrade682088
B · Outsource5065115

At a 25% reduction threshold for own operations, only B earns the bonus. It reduces that measure by 37.5% while increasing total emissions by 15%. For the combined measure, A reduces the baseline of 100 to 88. A therefore qualifies at a threshold of exactly 12%. B fails, and any threshold above 12% pays neither proposal. A payment record alone cannot verify the underlying emissions.

Nim Lab

Two players take turns removing one or more counters from a single heap. Taking the last counter wins. Combine the heap sizes with bitwise exclusive OR, written ⊕. The result is called the nim-sum. A player who can leave zero after each move can force a win.

Compare two moves. With heaps of 1, 3 and 5, the nim-sum is 7. Reduce 5 to 2 and the result is 1 ⊕ 3 ⊕ 2 = 0. Reduce 5 to 1 instead and the result is 3, giving the other player a winning reply. The proof starts with the highest binary place at which the nim-sum is 1.

Reversi Endgame Lab

On an eight-by-eight board, a legal placement brackets at least one uninterrupted line of opposing discs with a friendly disc at the far end. The move flips each bracketed line. In the lab, play begins from reachable late-game positions, where the remaining sequence of moves can be searched to the end.

With the score measured as the final disc difference for one side, that side chooses the largest continuation value and its opponent chooses the smallest. If a player has no legal move, that player must pass. If neither player can move, the current disc count settles the score even when squares remain empty. This minimax rule gives the exact value of the position under optimal play. An immediate gain in discs can still give the opponent a better reply.

Three proof puzzles

Lattice Dash asks for a shortest route. Breadth-first search explores all positions at one distance before going farther. In Target Forge, fractions can unlock an integer answer. Using 7, 7, 8 and 9 exactly once gives 7 × (9 − 8/7) = 55. In Parity Lights, pressing the same switch twice cancels its effect. That reduces the search to sets of switches.

Check a result

These public working papers document mathematical arguments and software behaviour. Their worked positions can be replayed. Claims about player enjoyment or learning gains would require evidence from players.

From a checkout of Atlas Open Mathematics, run python3 tools/verify_arcade_whitepapers.py with Python 3.10 or later. It independently counts completions for two fixed Sudoku openings and checks the fictional Boardroom figures and bonus thresholds. The Nim and Reversi papers give their own verification procedures.
