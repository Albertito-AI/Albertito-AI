    How the balance picks a reading

    Before each inspection, several labels may still fit the evidence. The lab chooses the reading that keeps the most labels possible. The seed settles equal choices. Every reading remains consistent with one fixed heavier parcel. Your job is to make even the least informative reading useful.

    Reveal the inspection bound

    One weighing has three possible readings. Two weighings can distinguish at most 9 labels, three at most 27. In general, k weighings can distinguish at most 3ᵏ labels. To guarantee identification among N parcels, choose the smallest integer k with 3ᵏ ≥ N.

    The bound is attainable. With m possible parcels, set g to the nearest integer to m/3. Weigh g possible parcels against g others and leave the rest off. Each possible group then has at most ⌈m/3⌉ members. Repeat on the group matching the reading. For one remaining parcel, no more weighing is needed.

    Try the inspection at a table

    Give eight identical opaque envelopes to a partner. Ask them to put the same number of identical counters in each, then add one extra counter to just one envelope. With a balance sensitive enough to show the difference, record your weighings and your identification. Repeat with the heavier envelope moved. Which inspection plan would you use before accepting a 12-envelope delivery?
