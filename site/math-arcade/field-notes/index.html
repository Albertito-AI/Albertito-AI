Lattice Dash

Shortest paths

Can you do it in one jump?

Draw a grid with both axes numbered 0, 1, 2. Start at (0, 0) and aim for (2, 1). A jump adds either (1, 0) or (1, 1) to your position, or subtracts one of them. Landings must stay on the grid. What is the fewest jumps you need?

Reveal the shortest route

The only legal first landings are (1, 0) and (1, 1). From (1, 0), adding (1, 1) reaches (2, 1). The target first appears after two jumps, so two is the minimum.

This is breadth-first search in miniature. List new positions reachable in one jump, then two, then three. Each jump costs one move, so the first layer containing the target gives the shortest distance.

Target Forge

Exact fractions

Make 24 from 3, 3, 8 and 8

Use each tile exactly once. At each move, combine two tiles by addition, subtraction, multiplication or division and put their result back. Can you leave 24 as the last tile?

Reveal the three moves
  1. Divide one 8 by one 3 to make 8/3.
  2. Subtract that fraction from the other 3 to make 9/3 − 8/3 = 1/3.
  3. Divide the remaining 8 by 1/3. Eight wholes contain 24 thirds.

The full expression is 8 ÷ (3 − 8/3) = 24. The denominator of 3 keeps the intermediate value exact, allowing the last division to land precisely on the target.

Parity Lights

Even and odd

Which light gets flipped twice?

Draw a 3 × 3 board with rows A, B, C and columns 1, 2, 3. Light up A1, A3, B1 and B3. Pressing a cell flips it and its neighbours immediately above, below, left and right. Predict the final board after pressing A1 and A3.

Reveal what happens

A1 flips A1, A2 and B1. A3 flips A3, A2 and B3. Each of the four lit cells flips once and goes dark. A2 flips twice and returns to dark. All other cells stay dark.

Parity means whether a count is even or odd. A light changes its final state exactly when it is flipped an odd number of times. That is why reversing the order of the two presses gives the same result.
