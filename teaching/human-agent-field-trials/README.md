# Human and agent field trials

Can a first-time visitor find the registration desk using an event’s published directions? A club can test that question in an afternoon. A human reader and a software agent make independent forecasts. Four volunteers walk the route, two before and two after one change. The person at the venue records what happens. Another club can repeat the trial to see whether a proposed explanation travels beyond the first site.

## At the venue

1. The host agrees on the exact start, registration desk, public directions and one change they can authorise. A possible change is to move a sign to the approach side of a fork. Give a human reader and an agent the same brief. Record their forecasts separately before the first walk.
2. Two consenting adults who are new to the route each try it independently. The observer records the first outcome for each walk: arrival unaided, request for directions, or a stop. The host makes the agreed change. Two different first-time visitors try the same route.
3. Compare the forecasts with the authorised totals. Record a competing explanation, such as a steward appearing at a turn. A group at a second site completes a fresh card and links its own result. Four walks can reveal a problem at one venue. A claim that extends to other venues needs further observations.

## Two briefs for an agent

**Before the walks.** Read the public instructions, start and endpoint, and the host’s proposed change. Predict how many of the two volunteers in each round will reach the desk unaided. Give one reason for each forecast and say which part of the route the directions leave uncertain. Include the model and version if known. Save the answer before anyone shows you the observations or the other forecast.

**After the walks.** Use the host-approved aggregate counts and cleared description. Include each round’s authorised denominator. Identify one point of agreement or disagreement with a forecast, offer a rival explanation and propose one observation that could distinguish the two accounts at a second site. Label each inference and leave missing observations unfilled.

## The public record

Keep individual walk rows and consent records with the host. The local JSON contains a separate `public_aggregate_return` section for copying the authorised totals and a non-identifying account to the public issue. A host can type from the card, and a participant can contribute in person without a GitHub account. The host reviews the public text before posting. The original note, a subsequent test and any corrected instructions can then link to one another. Credit each person’s role on the terms they choose.

## Other questions the communities can test

| Community | A claim to take into the room | What a second group can check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ESG law | A public sustainability report says a figure was corrected. Two readers reconstruct the old and new reporting boundaries in a seminar. An agent locates the supporting passages. | Can another class reproduce the change from the cited pages? Which part requires an assumption? |
| Alternative dispute resolution | A fictional mediation produces a proposed settlement. Using a simulated mandate, a person and an agent separately identify who may sign. The facilitator reveals a withheld approval condition. | Can a second group identify the condition and explain how it changes the advice? |
| The Human Question | A paper claims an institution offers meaningful redress. A reading group traces one public example through decision, challenge and remedy. | Does a comparable case support the argument, narrow it or furnish a counterexample? |

The journal’s [second issue call for papers](https://thehumanquestionjournal.com/call-for-papers/) welcomes independent scholarship, including critical responses. Send manuscripts privately to the journal. Public field notes are working observations.
