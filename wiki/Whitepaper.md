# Albertito-AI: a public research practice people can inspect and use

## Abstract

An inspectable public research portfolio needs clear routes from a claim to its source, version, observed result and limitation. This paper explains how the Albertito-AI hub approaches that problem and examines a recorded two-document extraction pilot in which review exposed answer leakage. It distinguishes documentation, software checks and proposed user evaluation. Readers seeking an example can start with the worked case; reviewers can follow the evidence map and correction history. No general efficacy or external validation is established.

## Purpose of the portfolio

This repository is a public entrance to Albert Tan Sin Hon's research, tools, teaching and creative work. Its deeper purpose is coherence: scholarship should inform useful methods, those methods should become inspectable artifacts, and experience with the artifacts should generate better research questions.

The thesis is that a public intellectual practice becomes more valuable when visitors can follow a claim into its sources, try a bounded demonstration, understand its limits and contribute a substantive correction. Reputation should emerge from useful, answerable work rather than from activity counts or inflated promises.

Prepared for Albert Tan Sin Hon with AI assistance on 2 November 2026. This is an interpretive design paper for the public hub. It does not replace authored scholarship, imply institutional endorsement or claim independent evaluation of the portfolio's experimental projects.

## First principles: visibility is not verification

A project being public does not make it validated, adopted or open-source. A paper link does not make a software implementation an evaluated research result. An attractive demonstration does not establish that a user completed the task independently. The hub's first responsibility is to keep these distinctions easy to see.

The second principle is provenance. A catalogue entry should identify the work, its author or contributor, source location, version and review status. A reader should be able to distinguish inspected source material from a metadata-only research lead. Corrections should preserve enough history to explain what changed.

The third principle is practical navigation. A visitor usually arrives with a task, not with knowledge of the portfolio's structure. “Trace a claim,” “create a short video” or “inspect a fictional submission check” is a more useful route than a long undifferentiated list of repositories. A good entrance connects a task to a concrete first action and its limitations.

## Fundamental logic of the public hub

The current profile and Research Lab connect scholarship, project demonstrations, teaching routes, a source-linked catalogue and verification records. The catalogue also supports programmatic reading. The guide follows curated routes rather than claiming to be an autonomous research authority.

This creates a small knowledge cycle: a research question motivates a tool; a tool produces a reproducible example; a visitor inspects or tries it; an observed difficulty becomes a correction or new question. Each transition should retain evidence. The hub should not infer user benefit from the existence of the last artifact in that chain.

The projects address related but distinct problems. RegNav makes source relationships inspectable; Source Watch notices changes in recorded dependencies; Court Submission Integrity offers bounded preflight checks; Symbolon and Piepowder examine machine-work agreements and review; Tallystick studies corrected observations and replay. Creative and reflective tools address different aspects of human capability. Their shared discipline is explicit assumptions and reviewable work, not a claim that one architecture solves every domain.

## Why the portfolio should remain plural

Coherence does not require collapsing every project into a single product. Different domains have different authority, privacy, licensing and validation requirements. A legal evidence tool, a creative renderer and a speculative scientific model should not inherit one another's assurance claims merely because they share a maintainer.

Integration should therefore be explicit and task-driven. An adapter should state what it carries, which fields are lost and what permissions it does not transfer. A public teaching example should not reveal private examinations or participant records. A commercial service should not silently relicense a research artifact. Useful boundaries are part of the architecture of the portfolio.

The same principle applies to AI assistance. Generated implementation, internal checks, independent agent review and qualified human review are different contributions. Attribution should describe what actually happened. A public hub earns trust by making those distinctions ordinary rather than exceptional.

## Aim and hope: institutions that can explain themselves

The near-term aim is a clear route from interest to useful engagement: reading a source, reproducing a result, reporting a defect, trying a teaching exercise or proposing a scoped collaboration. The larger hope is to strengthen institutions' ability to explain how evidence becomes a decision and how affected people can question that decision.

This aspiration connects law, sustainability, education and technology without claiming that software alone supplies accountability. People need access, expertise, time, rights and responsive institutions. A tool can reduce a particular friction while leaving those wider conditions unresolved.

Public benefit should be judged at the point of use. Does a researcher identify a qualification they would otherwise miss? Does a student better distinguish a source from an inference? Does a creator produce a useful communication they can revise? Does a reviewer understand why a finding remains unknown? These are more meaningful questions than how many files were published.

| Dependable catalogue | Versioned sources, rights and review metadata | Link checks, provenance checks and recorded corrections |
| Useful teaching | Exercises that develop transferable judgement | Actual learner observations and appropriate evaluation design |
| Independent contribution | Reproductions and critiques beyond the author | Permissioned records, attribution and substantive responses |
| Sustainable stewardship | Maintained projects with realistic obligations | Support evidence, funding clarity and succession plans |

The ideal wishlist includes accessible and multilingual explanations, stable citation exports, reproducibility bundles, better source-change maintenance, clear contribution routes and long-lived archival records. Project whitepapers should explain foundations and aspirations while linking to exact implementation evidence. They should evolve when a premise changes, not simply accumulate promotional prose.

Funding can sustain maintenance and useful work, but should not purchase favourable research outcomes or misleading endorsements. Public invitations should set clear scope, rights and expectations. A proposal, a paid engagement, an accepted delivery and repeat demand must remain separate records.

## Measuring success and preserving dissent

Measure whether visitors find the right entry, understand the project's status and complete a meaningful task. Record abandonment, misunderstanding and inaccessible paths. A useful catalogue should make limitations easier to find, not bury them beneath optimistic summaries.

At portfolio level, examine maintenance burden, unresolved defects, independently reproduced work and user outcomes. Keep negative findings and reasoned objections. Retire or archive work that no longer has a justified purpose rather than creating cosmetic activity to make every project appear equally alive.

The best case is a durable public practice in which scholarship, tools and teaching improve one another. People should leave with a clearer understanding, a usable artifact or a better question, and with enough evidence to disagree constructively. That is a practical contribution to society's capacity for collective learning.

### Method and evidence boundaries

The review used a purposive selection of repository records: the public README, machine-readable catalogue, research-pilot README and report, the pilot's evaluation history, fictional RegNav fixture expectations and the Wiki publisher. These materials were selected because they directly bear on the paper's claims about navigation, provenance, reproducibility and correction. This is not a systematic literature review or an exhaustive evaluation of every linked project. It provides no new findings about current law, learner outcomes or market demand.

Source inspection supports narrow statements about what a file contains. A recorded experiment supports a statement about its recorded inputs and conditions. Neither establishes that the same result will occur on unseen inputs. Where this paper proposes an evaluation or offers an interpretation, it identifies that status rather than reporting the proposal as completed work.

| Kind of statement | Sufficient evidence for the narrow claim | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A project is discoverable | An accessible entry with a resolvable source link | Source correctness or user adoption |
| A source was inspected | A named revision, inspected material and review scope | Independent expertise or exhaustive review |
| A demonstration ran | Inputs, environment, command and observed output | Real-world effectiveness or general accuracy |
| A reviewer corrected a defect | The original record, reason, change and follow-up evidence | Human peer review unless that actually occurred |
| A Wiki paper is published | Readable destination bytes matched to the intended source | Research validation, licensing permission or encyclopaedic notability |
| A project helps users | Appropriate observations from actual users and a defensible comparison | A benefit inferred solely from a fixture or page view |

### Conceptual foundations and prior practice

The design draws on established concerns about provenance and reusable research objects. W3C's PROV family describes the objects, activities and responsible agents involved in producing information. That vocabulary helps ask what was used, what changed and who took responsibility. It does not itself certify a source or settle the truth of a claim. The portfolio's records can be discussed using those concepts, but this paper reports no PROV conformance assessment.[1](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/NOTE-prov-overview-20130430/)

The FAIR principles emphasise findability, accessibility, interoperability and reusability, including identifiers, metadata, explicit usage rights and provenance. They provide questions for catalogue design. They do not make all material unrestricted: access can include authentication and authorisation. Here they are an analytical reference, not a FAIR certification or a claim that every catalogue field satisfies every principle.[2](https://www.gofair.foundation/fair-principles)

The portfolio-specific choice is to carry these concerns into a public teaching route: from a readable claim to a named artifact, a bounded observation and a visible limitation. A DOI can aid identification; an archive can preserve a version; a licence can specify permitted reuse. Those functions remain distinct from methodological quality. The same separation matters when one maintainer connects several projects.

## Worked example: learning from an evaluation defect

The repository's two-document research pilot is a useful case because it preserves a correction that qualifies its apparent success. Its recorded corpus comprises a four-page Singapore economic release and a two-page Bank of Thailand statement, both historical documents in English. The pilot selected sixteen content and location references. It compared Docling native extraction, pdfplumber and a combined route. The report records joint results of 14/16, 16/16 and 16/16 respectively.[5][6]

These numbers have a precise denominator. They refer to the selected checks, not all text on the six pages, Thai-language extraction, scanned documents or unseen material. The report's content test checks selected values or ordered tokens; its location test checks the physical page and whether a returned box's centre falls inside a manually marked region. It does not measure full semantic equivalence or exact box overlap.

The educational issue is the original date selector. Version 1 embedded the expected date in the selector used to find that same date. A successful match could therefore partly reflect information already supplied to the system. A separate agent's review identified this answer leakage. Version 2 instead locates a standalone date immediately after the issuing organisation's signature. The historical record expressly states that this correction occurred after outcomes had been observed, and that the annotations were not independently relabelled by a human.[7]

| Stage | Recorded action | What a careful reader learns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Define the target | Select content and page regions, retaining the original reference | A target needs an explicit rule and an inspectable version |
| Run the comparison | Preserve first-pass and corrected results | A final score alone hides development decisions |
| Examine the evaluator | Identify the date embedded in its own selector | Test logic can make a result look stronger than it is |
| Repair the selector | Use the corporate signature and structural date position | A correction should remove the identified dependency |
| Challenge the repair | Change the input date; remove the signature | The locator should return the changed date and reject the missing anchor |
| Retain the qualification | Label the revised method as post-outcome development | Repair does not retroactively create held-out validation |

Three further controls substitute a wrong value, wrong page and wrong cell region; the report records rejection in each case. These controls are valuable because they distinguish particular failure modes. They do not quantify every possible failure. The same assistant performed the original annotation and evaluation, so a wider evaluation should introduce independent annotation, a declared disagreement process and genuinely unseen documents.

A reader wishing to reproduce the pilot should use its pinned [README][5] and environment instructions rather than copy commands from a portfolio overview. First inspect the source notices and permitted public inputs. Record the tool and reference versions. Preserve any changed source-hash rejection, extraction failure or disagreement instead of forcing the expected result. This review does not claim that reproduction was newly performed.

The warranted conclusion is modest and useful: this recorded case demonstrates how a local research workflow can preserve source-linked observations and disclose a methodological correction. It provides no accuracy-based reason to replace pdfplumber on those files and no evidence of superior extraction on a broader population.[6]

## Teaching guide: from inspection to reasoned criticism

After working through the records, a learner should be able to identify the unit of evidence, separate content from location checking, explain answer leakage and state a conclusion at the scale the evidence supports.

1. **Locate the claim.** Find the reported 16/16 result. Identify its method, denominator, document set and date. Explain why “perfect PDF extraction” would exceed that evidence.
2. **Inspect a counterexample.** Explain how a correct numeric value at the wrong table location can mislead a reader. Relate it to the distinct wrong-value and wrong-region controls.
3. **Explain the correction.** Compare the v1 and v2 date-selection accounts. State why removing answer leakage improves the mechanism while leaving post-outcome development bias unresolved.
4. **Propose an evaluation.** Describe an unseen document set, an annotation procedure, disagreement handling and failure reporting. Declare which decisions must be fixed before observing results.
5. **Transfer the lesson.** Read the fictional RegNav expectations. The `irrelevant-pinpoint` case still has exit code zero and no structural error, while warnings remain. Explain why locating an existing section is a different question from whether that section supports the proposition.[8]

An adequate answer cites the record, gives a reason and states a limit. A stronger answer identifies a plausible counterexample and proposes a test capable of exposing it. A response that merely repeats a passing score has not demonstrated the intended judgement. These are proposed learning objectives and assessment prompts; learner effectiveness has not been measured by this review.

## Failure modes, maintenance and future evaluation

The hub can fail even when every file is readable. Readers may confuse a catalogue entry with an endorsement, a literal match with semantic support, a maintainer review with external peer review, or a current link with a current research conclusion. Dense navigation can also hide limitations that are technically present. These are concrete risks to investigate, not measured incidence rates.

A future first-use study should define the reader group and task, record both completion and misunderstanding, and explain how assistance is counted. For example, ask participants to find the pilot's strongest defensible claim and one limitation. Record whether they reach the source, understand the denominator and notice the post-outcome correction. Time-to-completion is secondary to those substantive outcomes. Publication of participant material requires an appropriate consent and privacy arrangement; this paper creates none.

Maintenance should preserve a dated claim register rather than overwrite earlier results. For an important claim, record the source revision, evidence location, review type, unresolved limitation and next event that would require rechecking. A change to a supporting source should reopen the relevant judgement. An inaccessible external link should be reported as inaccessible, without treating that observation as proof that the underlying work never existed.

The practical limit remains institutional. Documentation can help a reader find and contest a reason, but it does not supply rights of appeal, independent expertise, resources or a responsive decision-maker. The portfolio's public-benefit aspiration therefore remains a proposition to examine through actual use.
