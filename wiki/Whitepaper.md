# Albertito-AI: a public research practice people can inspect and use

## Purpose of the portfolio

This repository is a public entrance to Albert Tan Sin Hon's research, tools, teaching and creative work. Its deeper purpose is coherence: scholarship should inform useful methods, those methods should become inspectable artifacts, and experience with the artifacts should generate better research questions.

The thesis is that a public intellectual practice becomes more valuable when visitors can follow a claim into its sources, try a bounded demonstration, understand its limits and contribute a substantive correction. Reputation should emerge from useful, answerable work rather than from activity counts or inflated promises.

Prepared for Albert Tan Sin Hon with AI assistance on 2 November 2026. This is an interpretive design paper for the public hub. It does not replace authored scholarship, imply institutional endorsement or claim independent evaluation of the portfolio's experimental projects.

## First principles: visibility is not verification

A project being public does not make it validated, adopted or open-source. A paper link does not make a software implementation an evaluated research result. An attractive demonstration does not establish that a user completed the task independently. The hub's first responsibility is to keep these distinctions easy to see.

The second principle is provenance. A catalogue entry should identify the work, its author or contributor, source location, version and review status. A reader should be able to distinguish inspected source material from a metadata-only research lead. Corrections should preserve enough history to explain what changed.

The third principle is practical navigation. A visitor usually arrives with a task, not with knowledge of the portfolio's structure. “Trace a claim,” “create a short video” or “inspect a fictional submission check” is a more useful route than a long undifferentiated list of repositories. A good entrance connects a task to a concrete first action and its limitations.

## Fundamental logic of the public hub

The current profile and Research Lab connect scholarship, project demonstrations, teaching routes, a source-linked catalogue and verification records. The catalogue also supports programmatic reading. The guide follows curated routes rather than claiming to be an autonomous research authority.

This creates a small knowledge cycle: a research question motivates a tool; a tool produces a reproducible example; a visitor inspects or tries it; an observed difficulty becomes a correction or new question. Each transition should retain evidence. The hub should not infer user benefit from the existence of the last artifact in that chain.

The projects address related but distinct problems. RegNav makes source relationships inspectable; Source Watch notices changes in recorded dependencies; Court Submission Integrity offers bounded preflight checks; Symbolon and Piepowder examine machine-work agreements and review; Tallystick studies corrected observations and replay. Creative and reflective tools address different aspects of human capability. Their shared discipline is explicit assumptions and reviewable work, not a claim that one architecture solves every domain.

## Why the portfolio should remain plural

Coherence does not require collapsing every project into a single product. Different domains have different authority, privacy, licensing and validation requirements. A legal evidence tool, a creative renderer and a speculative scientific model should not inherit one another's assurance claims merely because they share a maintainer.

Integration should therefore be explicit and task-driven. An adapter should state what it carries, which fields are lost and what permissions it does not transfer. A public teaching example should not reveal private examinations or participant records. A commercial service should not silently relicense a research artifact. Useful boundaries are part of the architecture of the portfolio.

The same principle applies to AI assistance. Generated implementation, internal checks, independent agent review and qualified human review are different contributions. Attribution should describe what actually happened. A public hub earns trust by making those distinctions ordinary rather than exceptional.

## Aim and hope: institutions that can explain themselves

The near-term aim is a clear route from interest to useful engagement: reading a source, reproducing a result, reporting a defect, trying a teaching exercise or proposing a scoped collaboration. The larger hope is to strengthen institutions' ability to explain how evidence becomes a decision and how affected people can question that decision.

This aspiration connects law, sustainability, education and technology without claiming that software alone supplies accountability. People need access, expertise, time, rights and responsive institutions. A tool can reduce a particular friction while leaving those wider conditions unresolved.

Public benefit should be judged at the point of use. Does a researcher identify a qualification they would otherwise miss? Does a student better distinguish a source from an inference? Does a creator produce a useful communication they can revise? Does a reviewer understand why a finding remains unknown? These are more meaningful questions than how many files were published.

## Ideal progression and wishlist

| Direction | Desired improvement | Evidence gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear public entrance | Task-based routes with honest status | First-use observations and repaired navigation problems |
| Dependable catalogue | Versioned sources, rights and review metadata | Link checks, provenance checks and recorded corrections |
| Useful teaching | Exercises that develop transferable judgement | Actual learner observations and appropriate evaluation design |
| Independent contribution | Reproductions and critiques beyond the author | Permissioned records, attribution and substantive responses |
| Sustainable stewardship | Maintained projects with realistic obligations | Support evidence, funding clarity and succession plans |

The ideal wishlist includes accessible and multilingual explanations, stable citation exports, reproducibility bundles, better source-change maintenance, clear contribution routes and long-lived archival records. Project whitepapers should explain foundations and aspirations while linking to exact implementation evidence. They should evolve when a premise changes, not simply accumulate promotional prose.

Funding can sustain maintenance and useful work, but should not purchase favourable research outcomes or misleading endorsements. Public invitations should set clear scope, rights and expectations. A proposal, a paid engagement, an accepted delivery and repeat demand must remain separate records.

## Measuring success and preserving dissent

Measure whether visitors find the right entry, understand the project's status and complete a meaningful task. Record abandonment, misunderstanding and inaccessible paths. A useful catalogue should make limitations easier to find, not bury them beneath optimistic summaries.

At portfolio level, examine maintenance burden, unresolved defects, independently reproduced work and user outcomes. Keep negative findings and reasoned objections. Retire or archive work that no longer has a justified purpose rather than creating cosmetic activity to make every project appear equally alive.

The best case is a durable public practice in which scholarship, tools and teaching improve one another. People should leave with a clearer understanding, a usable artifact or a better question, and with enough evidence to disagree constructively. That is a practical contribution to society's capacity for collective learning.
